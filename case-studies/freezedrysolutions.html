Case Study

Cold email, live paid site in under a week, before she left for a trade show.

Freeze-drying equipment and co-manufacturing, Fortunata LLC. A hard five-day deadline, two distinct buyer paths on one page, and zero room to miss the launch date.

Tracee McAfee, Co-Founder/CEO

The Client

A founder launching a second brand into a wider market, on a deadline she couldn't move.

Tracee McAfee runs Cryo Cure, a curing equipment brand serving the cannabis post-harvest space, and was launching Freeze Dry Solutions, a sister brand extending that same technology into a much wider market: food, pet food, nutraceutical, and pharma freeze-drying customers. She needed a page live before she boarded a flight to a trade show. The date wasn't flexible.

The Problem

A brand-new page, two different buyers, and five days to get it right.

The page had to serve two completely different audiences in one place: buyers looking to purchase a commercial freeze dryer outright, and buyers who wanted to outsource the freeze-drying process entirely. Get that wrong and one path reads as the "real" offer while the other gets ignored, causing confusion and lost leads on both sides.

The brand itself carried its own risk: a "no white on blue" instruction could easily have pushed the palette off her actual brand, or made the page hard to read for buyers seeing it for the first time at a trade show. And with imagery, precision mattered more than it usually does: food, nutraceutical, and pharma buyers know the difference between freeze-dried and simply dehydrated, and getting that wrong would have undercut credibility with the exact audience the page needed to convince. All of it had to go from a cold outreach conversation to a fully live site in under a week.

The Strategy

Built fast, refined precisely.

A discovery call on Wednesday led to a working preview within 24 hours. From there, the build went through focused rounds of revision rather than a single big reveal, catching and fixing the details that actually mattered to a buyer evaluating unfamiliar equipment.

The first color pass didn't land: an initial charcoal and steel-blue palette missed her brand's actual tone entirely. It was rebuilt around a lighter, frosty-blue palette that matched her existing brand, then verified for WCAG accessibility contrast so the copy stayed easy to read against every background.

The bigger issue was the imagery. Early stock photos of freeze-dried food read as simply dehydrated: plump, glossy, the wrong process entirely. For a buyer trying to understand what freeze-drying actually produces, that's not a small detail, it's misleading. Multiple rounds of sourcing went into getting the texture right against real reference photos: collapsed, cratered, matte: what freeze-dried fruit actually looks like, not a stand-in.

The equipment itself needed the same accuracy. Rather than generating images of freeze dryers that could misrepresent a real product she resells, the page used actual cutaway and interior shots sourced directly from the manufacturer's own product footage, with her permission, giving buyers a true sense of scale and build instead of a rendering that could set the wrong expectation.

Both buyer paths were built with equal visual weight throughout (same hierarchy, same CTA prominence, same clarity) with two separate lead forms so equipment inquiries and co-manufacturing inquiries stay cleanly segmented from the start.
